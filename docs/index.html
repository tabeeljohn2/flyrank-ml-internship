Ranking Content Refresh Candidates: A Grouped-Validation Baseline

TABEEL JOHN · FlyRank ML Internship · Research prototype, Week 7

Abstract

Editors have limited time, so which pages should they look at first? I asked whether search-performance signals can rank pages by their risk of a large impression drop. I used an anonymized FlyRank slice of 20,249 pages from 30 clients, and defined "declined" as the last 30 days falling below 70% of the previous 30 days. A logistic regression, validated by holding out whole clients, reached a mean AUC of 0.583 against 0.420 for a rule-based baseline, so it is directionally better but weak and unstable, and the signal appears to rest mostly on content age. I turn the scores into a ranked reading list with reason codes for a human editor, and I claim nothing about whether a refresh would help.

1. Introduction

A content team cannot review every page every week. A ranked queue could help an editor decide where to read first. This paper asks a narrow question: using only observed search metrics and page attributes, can we rank pages by the chance their impressions fall sharply, and is that ranking better than a simple rule? The output is decision support for a human, not an automated action.

2. Data

The data is the anonymized starter slice of the FlyRank ML Internship release (content_refresh_anonymized.csv): one row per page, 30,000 rows and 44 columns, covering 32 pseudonymized clients. Search metrics come from Google Search Console and traffic metrics from GA4. Metrics are aggregated over a trailing 90-day window ending at export time. The trend label uses the most recent 30 days against the 30 days before that (days 31 to 60 back). The data dictionary gives no calendar dates, so none are shown here. This paper does not use the full multi-million-row warehouse release.

  • Exclusion: pages with fewer than 50 impressions in the previous 30 days were dropped, because a ratio on a tiny base is noisy.
  • Result: 20,249 pages from 30 clients. The observed decline rate is 0.521.
  • Public safety: clients and pages appear only as anonymized IDs, and none are shown here.

3. Methodology

Label. declined = 1 when impressions_last_30d / impressions_prev_30d < 0.7. This is an observed drop over one window, not the effect of any action.

Features. Numeric: days since last update, content age (days), word count, search volume, competition, CPC (median-imputed, standardized). Categorical: content type, main intent (most-frequent imputation, one-hot). Word count is missing for 29.5% of pages. Left out on purpose: the trend fields (trend_pct, trend_direction), because the label comes from them, and client_id, which is used only for grouping, never as a feature.

Models. Logistic regression with balanced class weights, and a random forest (200 trees, depth 8) for comparison. The baseline is the Week 4 rule.

Validation. Grouped cross-validation by client_id, so no client appears in both training and test. Probabilities are out-of-fold. This is the leakage check: a random split would let a client's pages sit on both sides and overstate the score.

Assumptions I chose: the 0.7 threshold, the archetype cut-offs, and 2 editor-hours per page.

5. Limitations and honest framing

  • Signal strength is weak and unstable across splits (AUC 0.455 to 0.876).
  • The label is an observed drop over one window. Nothing here shows that a refresh would recover traffic, and the score is not a cause.
  • Only 30 clients. Results may not transfer to a new client without re-validating on held-out clients.
  • No seasonality handling, no time-based validation, no future-window check.
  • Thresholds, archetype cut-offs and editor-hours are my assumptions.
  • Word count is median-imputed for 29.5% of pages, so its effect is weakly measured.
  • "Impressions lost in the picks" is an observed drop, not recoverable upside.

6. Ranked recommendations: the action playbook

Pages are ranked by modeled decline risk. Conditions apply in order, and the first match sets the reason code. An editor reads every REVIEW page before any change. The queue is a reading list, not an instruction.

Do not automate: publishing, rewriting or deleting pages, or changing titles and redirects from a score; promising a client that a refresh will recover traffic; ranking editors or clients with these scores; or applying the queue to a new client without re-validation.

Retrain triggers: decline base rate moves more than 10 points; grouped AUC falls below 0.55; top-N is not above the base rate; feature missing rates rise more than 10 points; new clients appear.

7. Reproducibility

  • Repository: github.com/tabeeljohn2/flyrank-ml-internship
  • Notebooks in work/notebooks/: capstone (open in Colab) and action playbook (open in Colab)
  • Outputs: work/outputs/ (queue CSV and metrics JSON); figure in work/figures/
  • Run in Google Colab with scikit-learn, pandas and numpy. Grouped validation uses GroupKFold (5 folds in the playbook notebook). The random forest uses seed 42. To rerun, open the notebook in Colab and choose Runtime, then Run all; it clones the repo and reads the bundled CSV.
